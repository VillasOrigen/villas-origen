Getting to Las Terrenas: airports, flights and transfers

The airport for Las Terrenas is El Catey (AZS), 25 minutes away on a brand-new highway — not Punta Cana, 4 hours away. Seasonal direct flights link Canada to El Catey in just over 4 hours; from other markets, you generally fly into Santo Domingo (a 2 to 2.5-hour drive). We make this trip several times a year — here is everything you need to know.

By the hosts of Villas Origen, in CosónUpdated August 2026

Which airport should you choose for Las Terrenas?

Five international airports can serve as your gateway, but they are far from equal. El Catey (AZS), on the Samaná peninsula, is the right choice whenever a flight goes there: 25 minutes by road to Las Terrenas. Santo Domingo (SDQ) is the serious alternative. Puerto Plata, La Romana and Punta Cana mean long drives — worth it only for unbeatable fares.

Are there direct flights to El Catey?

Yes. From Canada, seasonal direct flights reach El Catey in about 4 h to 4 h 20 — we take them ourselves on every stay. Departure cities and airlines change from season to season: check what is flying when you book; the schedule grows every year.

From Europe — where a large share of Las Terrenas visitors come from — you generally land in Santo Domingo (SDQ), then drive 2 to 2.5 hours: Paris, Madrid, Frankfurt and Rome all have nonstop links (about a 9-hour flight). From the United States, New York, Miami and several East Coast cities serve SDQ nonstop.

What is the drive from El Catey like?

It is the pleasant surprise of the arrival: a brand-new highway in excellent condition, lit at night — a real 25 minutes, timed on every one of our trips. The road climbs through the mountains and opens onto genuine viewpoints over the sea and the coast.

If you land in daylight, two stops are worth it: the lookout points high above the coast, with the beach below, and the Cueva Boulevard del Atlántico, a stalactite-lined cave right by the road. Your holiday starts on the drive.

Private transfer or taxi: how to choose?

Our most important advice, learned the hard way: book a referred transfer company rather than a taxi found on the spot. On our first stay, a non-referred driver did the trip at a speed that frightened the whole family. Since then we only work with HugXander Transport, which we recommend to our own guests: calm driving, air-conditioned vehicle, punctual and warm drivers.

The logistics follow: five passengers and about ten bags all fitted without difficulty — the team matches the vehicle to the group. Book before your flight, and book the return at the same time.

Should you rent a car at the airport?

It is not compulsory, and it is not our formula. We prefer to arrive by transfer, then rent in the village — a quad for the day or a car depending on the outings planned. That way you avoid driving at night on unfamiliar roads on arrival day, and you only pay for a vehicle on the days you use it.

For daily life from Cosón, a vehicle remains recommended: the grocery store and the centre are a five-minute drive. Our guide Where to stay in Las Terrenas details what can be done on foot in each area.

El Catey airport in practice

El Catey is a small airport, and that is a luxury: few flights, so no crowds. On both of our departures to Canada, check-in and security took under 30 minutes. Arrivals are just as quick — the contrast with the big tourist airports is striking.

The flip side: with few flights, you don't really choose your arrival time. Ours have always been in the late afternoon, which leaves time to settle in before dark.

Our arrival tips

To stay connected, an eSIM works very well: we used Airalo on each of our stays, activated before take-off — no queue at a counter, connected on landing.

Book the transfer before you leave, outbound and return together: your driver waits at the exit, and the departure is as smooth as the arrival.

Landing in daylight? Ask your driver to stop at the lookouts along the road — the view over the coast is the finest prologue a holiday can have.

Frequently asked questions about getting to Las Terrenas

What is the closest airport to Las Terrenas?

El Catey (AZS), Samaná's international airport, about 25 minutes from Las Terrenas on a brand-new highway. It is the right choice whenever a flight goes there; Santo Domingo (SDQ), a 2 to 2.5-hour drive, is the main alternative.

Are there direct flights to Las Terrenas?

The airport for Las Terrenas is El Catey (AZS): seasonal direct flights reach it from Canada in about 4 h to 4 h 20. From Europe, nonstop links target Santo Domingo — Paris, Madrid, Frankfurt and Rome — followed by a 2 to 2.5-hour drive; from the United States, New York and Miami serve SDQ nonstop. Schedules change: check when you book.

How much is a transfer from El Catey airport?

In 2026, with HugXander: US$85 one way for 1 to 5 people ($150 round trip) and US$115 for 6 to 8 ($210 round trip), per vehicle. Book before your flight, ideally outbound and return together.

How long is the drive from Santo Domingo to Las Terrenas?

Allow 2 to 2.5 hours from Las Américas airport (SDQ). Towards downtown Santo Domingo, traffic can stretch the trip to 3 hours — we have experienced it ourselves.

Can you rent a car on arrival?

Yes, but it is not essential at the airport. A proven formula: arrive by private transfer, then rent a quad or a car in the village on the days you need one. That way you avoid night driving on unfamiliar roads on arrival day.

Should you book your transfer in advance?

Yes, with a referred company, before your flight. A taxi found on the spot can hold bad surprises — we experienced dangerously fast driving ourselves. With a booked driver, someone waits for you at the exit and the trip is calm.
