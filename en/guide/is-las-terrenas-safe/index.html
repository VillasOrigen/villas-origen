Is Las Terrenas safe? The honest answer of a family who lives there

Yes: Las Terrenas is considered one of the safest destinations in the Dominican Republic, and after more than a month on site as a family, that is our experience too. It is the question we get asked most often before a first stay, and it deserves better than a reassuring brochure answer. Here is what the official sources say, what we actually live, and the one subject that in our view calls for real caution.

By the hosts of Villas Origen, in CosónUpdated September 2026

The short answer

Las Terrenas is one of the places in the Caribbean where we have felt safest. That is not a sales line: over more than a month on site, walking in town at night, dining out, leaving our things on the beach while swimming, we have never had a moment of worry. Las Terrenas is a touristy, international town with an expat community that settled decades ago, and you feel it in the everyday atmosphere.

Now the honest nuance. No destination is risk-free, the Dominican Republic varies a lot from one place to another, and there is one subject where caution is not optional: the road, at night. We come back to it below, because it is the most useful advice on this page.

What do governments say about safety in the Dominican Republic?

Let's play it fully transparent, because you will find these anyway.

The Government of Canada rates the whole Dominican Republic under "Exercise a high degree of caution", mainly because of petty crime (bag snatching in busy tourist areas) and above all road safety: the country has one of the highest road accident rates in the world, and Ottawa explicitly recommends not driving after dark.

France places the country under "reinforced vigilance" (yellow), advises against driving at night and suggests preferring ATMs located inside banks.

Two things to understand when reading these advisories. First, they cover the whole country, from Santo Domingo neighbourhoods to tourist areas, which do not live the same reality. Second, look at what they actually emphasise: the number one documented risk is not violent crime against tourists, it is the road. Our experience says exactly the same thing.

At the local level, independent indices point the same way: Las Terrenas ranks among the country's best-rated towns for safety, and a dedicated tourist police force (CESTUR) is present in visitor areas.

Can you walk at night in Las Terrenas? Our experience

Walking into town at night for dinner: no problem, ever. The central streets are lively, people are remarkably kind, and the atmosphere is that of a seaside village where everyone crosses paths.

A detail that struck us: our real estate agent, who grew up here, told us that as a teenager, walking home at night worried nobody, and that this is still the spirit of the town today. Long-time residents describe Las Terrenas as a community where violence against people is rare. Very concretely, that is also our feeling on every stay, including for the women of the family.

At the beach, we leave our things on the sand while we swim, keeping an eye on them and without displaying valuables, as we would on any beach in the world. In a cumulative month, never an issue.

Is driving dangerous in Las Terrenas? The real subject: the road at night

If there is one piece of advice to remember from this page, it is this one. It is also the one brochures never mention.

On weekends, and particularly Sunday nights, Las Terrenas is a party destination for many Dominicans from the region. It is joyful and musical (we talk about it in our beach field notes), but some of those partygoers then take the road home, and some drive under the influence. The dark main roads between villages, on weekend nights, are the one place where we apply a strict rule: we are not on them.

Concretely, here is our practice. In town, taking the car in the evening is fine: traffic is slow and distances are short. The main roads (towards the airport, El Limón, Sánchez), we drive them in daylight. And for arrival and departure, a transfer with a referred driver settles the question. Our guide Getting to Las Terrenas explains how we organise it.

What precautions should you take in Las Terrenas? (the same as at home)

Nothing exotic here. It is the list we would follow in any city in the world, and it is enough: we lock the villa's doors, valuables do not lie in plain sight when staff come to the house, and the phone does not wander around at arm's length in the street.

About money: governments advise ATMs inside banks, and it is a good travel habit. That said, in Las Terrenas, withdrawing at an outdoor ATM has never caused us the slightest problem. Make your withdrawals in daylight, keep a reasonable amount of cash in pesos rather than large sums, and you will be fine.

That's it. Honestly, the list is short because the reality is.

Is Las Terrenas safe for a family, and for women?

That is our case, and it is precisely why we built here. Children on the beach, dinners in the village, the markets: everything is lived with the same ease as a classic family destination. The Cosón area, residential and calm, adds a layer of tranquillity: you hear the palm fronds, not the party. The women of the family move around with the same ease, and the village format, where everyone crosses paths and recognises each other, has a lot to do with it. If your underlying question is "can I bring my family with peace of mind?", our lived answer is yes, with the common-sense reflexes above and the road rule at night.

Is Las Terrenas safer than the rest of the Dominican Republic?

National averages mix very different realities: the difficult neighbourhoods of Santo Domingo and the seaside villages do not live the same life. Las Terrenas combines the factors that work in its favour. A village scale where people recognise each other. An international community settled for decades (French, Italians, Canadians…). An economy that lives on tourism and protects it. The presence of the tourist police. That is what explains the gap between the general advisories on the country and how it actually feels once there, for us as for the visitors we meet.

Frequently asked questions about safety in Las Terrenas

Is Las Terrenas safe for tourists?

Yes. It is one of the best-rated destinations in the Dominican Republic for safety, with an established international community and a tourist police presence. Our family experience on site, over more than a cumulative month, points the same way. The useful precautions are those of any destination: common sense with valuables, and caution on the roads at night.

Can you walk at night in Las Terrenas?

In the centre and the lively streets, yes. We do it on every stay to go to dinner, without worry. As everywhere, avoid deserted areas and isolated beaches after dark.

Is it a safe destination for a family?

Yes. It is our case: the children, the beach, dinners in the village, everything is lived simply. The Cosón area, residential and calm, is particularly peaceful.

Is driving dangerous in the Dominican Republic?

It is the country's real point of vigilance: the road accident rate is among the highest in the world, and drunk driving is common on weekend nights. Our rule: the car in town without problem, the main roads in daylight only, and a referred driver for airport transfers.

Can you leave your things on the beach while swimming?

We do it at every swim, keeping an eye on them and without leaving valuables in plain sight. In a cumulative month on the area's beaches, never a problem.

Is Las Terrenas safe for a solo female traveller?

The testimony of local residents and our family experience point the same way: the village format, the international community and the lively streets make Las Terrenas particularly comfortable to live in, for women too. The usual solo travel reflexes apply: busy streets at night, referred taxis for long distances, no isolated beach after dark.
