Where to stay in Las Terrenas: the area guide

Las Terrenas breaks down into a handful of areas with very different characters: Punta Popy and Las Ballenas to do everything on foot, the town centre for the buzz, Playa Bonita for surfing, Cosón and El Portillo for peace and quiet. The right choice comes down to one question: do you want to sleep inside the atmosphere, or visit it whenever you feel like it? We stayed in several of these areas before settling in Cosón — here is what we wish we had read.

By the hosts of Villas Origen, in CosónUpdated August 2026

What are the areas of Las Terrenas?

The village stretches along the north coast of the Samaná peninsula. From east to west: El Portillo, the village beach around Punta Popy, the centre, Playa Las Ballenas, then the point of Punta Bonita and finally Playa Cosón. Everything is 5 to 20 minutes apart by car — so choosing your area mostly means choosing the atmosphere you come home to at night.

Punta Popy and Las Ballenas: everything on foot, in the buzz

We stayed between the two in March, and it is the perfect area for living outdoors: beach bars, small restaurants, shops, a morning run on the sand — and the town centre within walking distance. The atmosphere is joyful, lively, very Dominican.

The flip side is simple: at night, it lives. Neighbouring villas entertain, music can run late, and at weekends locals come to party on the beach, sound systems included. This is where you enjoy the atmosphere — not where you look for silence.

The town centre: structured chaos

That is the phrase we coined on the spot: structured chaos. Scooters everywhere, music, movement — and yet everything works, and people are remarkably kind.

It is also where the good deals are: small local restaurants are noticeably cheaper than the beachfront tables, and often excellent. Add the decoration and jewellery shops, and the centre becomes the best end-of-day stroll in Las Terrenas — whether you sleep there or not.

Playa Bonita: the surfers' beach

Playa Bonita is reached by car, on the far side of the point that separates it from Playa Cosón. It is the surfers' beach: the sea is calmer in the morning and the waves build in the afternoon — plan your swim accordingly.

Towards the point, the beach clubs are worth the stop: Casa Grande on the Bonita side, El Mosquito on the Cosón side. As a place to sleep, it is a quiet corner with no shops within walking distance — a car is essential.

Cosón: live the village by day, come home to peace

Cosón is the fastest-growing area of Las Terrenas, west of the village. The first bay, served by a small residential road, is the quietest: no crowds, no loudspeakers, and the beach a few minutes away on foot — we wrote a complete guide to Playa Cosón.

Let's be honest: at night, Cosón is not the neighbourhood of restaurants and shops. The right formula is the one we use ourselves — drive to the centre in five minutes, park, walk, soak up the atmosphere… then come home to sleep in silence. You live the village by day, and sleep in peace at night.

El Portillo: large homes and wild beaches

East of the village, El Portillo is Cosón's upscale cousin: large homes, plenty of calm, wilder beaches. No shops or restaurants within walking distance — this is an area you live in by car.

Along with Cosón, it is also one of the two areas where the beaches stay quiet at weekends, when the village beaches fill up.

The advice you won't read anywhere else

At weekends, pick your beach by the mood you want. On Saturdays and Sundays, locals party on Punta Popy and Las Ballenas — music, families, sound systems: joyful, but loud. For a peaceful beach on those days, head to Cosón or El Portillo.

The waterfront comes at a price. The same plate costs noticeably less three streets from the beach than on the front line. The best value hides in the small local restaurants of the centre.

On Sunday evenings, keep journeys short and well-lit. That is advice we were given locally, after the weekend festivities: avoid the long dark roads between villages that evening.

On safety, our experience is clear: over several stays, not one moment of worry, including on foot late at night in the village. Las Terrenas is a place where you stroll with peace of mind.

Frequently asked questions about Las Terrenas areas

What is the best area to stay in Las Terrenas?

It depends on the stay you want: Punta Popy and Las Ballenas to do everything on foot in the buzz, the town centre for immersion and lower prices, Playa Bonita for surfing, Cosón and El Portillo for calm and families. All areas are 5 to 20 minutes apart by car.

Where should I stay to do everything on foot?

Between Punta Popy and Las Ballenas: restaurants, beach bars, shops and the town centre are all within walking distance. It is the liveliest area — at night the music can run late, especially at weekends.

Where should I stay for peace and quiet in Las Terrenas?

In Cosón or El Portillo. The first bay of Cosón, served by a small residential road, is especially peaceful: no crowds or music, even in high season, and the beach stays quiet at weekends.

Is Las Terrenas safe at night?

In our experience over several stays, yes: we never felt unsafe, including walking late at night in the village. One piece of local advice: on Sunday evenings, keep journeys short and well-lit rather than taking long dark roads.

Do I need a car depending on the area?

Between Punta Popy, Las Ballenas and the centre, you can live very well on foot. In Cosón, El Portillo and Playa Bonita, a vehicle is recommended: the beach may be walkable, but restaurants and groceries are a drive away.

What is the difference between Cosón and Punta Popy?

Punta Popy is the village beach: lively, lined with restaurants, with kitesurfing and music. Cosón is the long wild beach to the west: very calm, limited access, ideal for walking and resting. At Popy you live outdoors; at Cosón you come home to peace.
