The best beaches around Las Terrenas: the hosts' field notes

The Samaná Peninsula gathers some of the most beautiful beaches in the Dominican Republic — and most of them are less than thirty minutes from Las Terrenas. We live part of the year in Cosón, a five-minute walk from the beach, and we explore them one by one, season after season. Here is our notebook: not a ranking copied from elsewhere, but what we have seen with our own eyes — distances, swimming conditions and access tips included. And for the only one we haven't visited yet, we tell you so, plainly.

By the hosts of Villas Origen, in CosónUpdated September 2026

Playa Cosón — ours, and the most spectacular

A 5-minute walk from the villa · immense, wild, lined with coconut palms

We are biased, but the guides agree with us: Cosón is regularly cited as the most beautiful beach in the area. Several kilometres of golden sand, very few buildings, coconut palms as far as the eye can see, and an end-of-day light like nothing else. This is the beach for long walks, sunsets and grilled-fish lunches with your feet in the sand.

An honest word about swimming, because some guides describe Cosón as "always rough": that is not what we experience. In March the sea was very calm; and over three weeks from late July to early August, not a single day did the waves keep us from swimming — nothing exhausting or worrying, unlike Bonita in the afternoon. Some days have more waves than others, of course, and winter can bring swell; watch the sea for a few minutes before going in, as everywhere on the north coast.

We wrote a full guide to it: Playa Cosón, the guide by those who live there.

Playa Bonita — the postcard (and its boardwalk)

~10 minutes by car · swim in the morning, surf in the afternoon

Bonita lives up to its name: pale sand, coconut palms leaning over the water, a few hotels and restaurants right on the beach. What we love most: the boardwalk along the beach — perfect for walking or running at sunrise, one of the loveliest morning atmospheres in Las Terrenas.

Our lived-in advice: swim in the morning. In the afternoon, for a good part of the year, the waves grow to the point of making swimming genuinely unpleasant — which is exactly what delights the surfers who take over. Two beaches in one, depending on the hour.

Playa Las Ballenas — the beach of local life

Right in Las Terrenas · restaurants and bars with your feet in the sand

Las Ballenas is the beach you go to for the atmosphere as much as the water: small restaurants and bars all along, a pleasant stroll on the road or on the sand, easy swimming. It's the beach we recommend for a car-free afternoon, stringing together beach, drinks and dinner.

Worth knowing: weekends are party time. The central beaches can be reached by car right up to the sand, and Dominican families settle in for the day — coolers, barbecue, big speakers and bachata. It's a joyful immersion in local life; if you're after quiet, come on a weekday (or stay in Cosón).

Punta Popy — the lively one

Right in Las Terrenas · kitesurf, restaurants, buzz

Next door to Las Ballenas, Punta Popy is the liveliest beach: kitesurfers, cafés, restaurants, locals and visitors mingling. Same remark as for Las Ballenas: superb on weekdays, very lively (and loud) on weekends. It's the beach to pick when you want something happening.

Playa El Portillo — the discreet one

~20 minutes by car · calm, uncrowded

We got there by a rather rocambolesque track — squeezed between the trees, wondering whether the car would fit. Check your route before setting off: there is a more civilised access via El Portillo's main road. The reward at the end: a beautiful, calm beach, nearly deserted on weekdays, with few waves in the afternoon when we visited. Honestly? It doesn't dethrone Cosón. But if you're staying on the east side or want one more quiet beach, it's worth the stop.

Playa Morón — the secret favourite

~30 minutes by car, towards El Limón · our advice: go

If there is one "hidden" beach to do during a stay, it's this one. Golden sand, remarkably blue water, almost nobody — and a large restaurant overlooking the whole bay, the only building in sight. The afternoon swim was superb when we visited, and we even found sand dollars along the shore (look at them, photograph them — leave them to the sea).

The access quirk that puzzles everyone: you arrive at a gate (a real-estate development is under construction in the area). Simply say you're going to the restaurant — and do go, the view is worth the meal. The road from Las Terrenas crosses the village of El Limón, lined with coconut palms; at the beach entrance, a statue of the Taíno queen Anacaona stands guard.

Playa Jackson — the boat expedition

Reachable only by sea · a half-day excursion

No road leads to Playa Jackson: it's a beach you earn, on a boat excursion from Las Terrenas with snorkelling stops on the way — count on half a day. We went with The Dive Academy, the Las Terrenas dive school, and the experience was impeccable.

The arrival is a shock: a deserted beach backed by vegetation so dense and tall you'd think you were in Jurassic Park. Walking along the shore, you see rivers coming down the mountain and flowing straight into the sea. Nobody, or almost. It's the excursion we recommend to anyone who wants to see the peninsula at its wildest.

Playa Rincón — the legend, at the end of the road

~1 h 15 by road · a full day · the beach all of Samaná talks about

Full transparency: this is the only beach in this notebook we haven't yet seen with our own eyes — it's far from Las Terrenas, and it sits at the very top of our list. But no guide to Samaná's beaches can skip it: Playa Rincón is regularly ranked among the most beautiful beaches in the Caribbean. Three kilometres of undeveloped sand and, at its far end, the Caño Frío — a startlingly cold freshwater river forming a natural pool. The warm-sea / icy-river contrast is its signature.

What to know: count on a full day from Las Terrenas (about 1 h 15 by road, the last section bumpy — take care after rain). A clever alternative: drive to Las Galeras and take a lancha (boat taxi, about twenty minutes). On site, small rustic restaurants grill the fish of the day; bring cash, there is no ATM. The centre of the beach is calm and family-friendly most of the year; the eastern end gets more waves. From January to March, combine it with whale watching in Samaná Bay.

Three tips that hold for every beach

Morning is almost always the best time: calmer sea, soft light, less wind. On the north coast the waves often build in the afternoon, especially from December to April.

Bring cash in pesos: beach restaurants, motoconchos, coconuts — almost nothing outside the centre takes cards.

Weekends belong to Dominican families on the drive-up beaches (Ballenas, Popy): joyful and musical. For absolute calm on a Sunday, choose Cosón, Morón or an excursion.

Frequently asked questions about the beaches of Las Terrenas

What is the most beautiful beach around Las Terrenas?

For us, Playa Cosón — immense, wild, and a five-minute walk from the villa. Playa Rincón is its great rival at the scale of the peninsula (count on a full day), and Playa Morón is the little-known favourite thirty minutes away.

Can you swim everywhere around Las Terrenas?

Yes, but the north coast faces the Atlantic: waves vary with the season and the hour. Watch the sea for a few minutes before going in. Las Ballenas and Punta Popy are the easiest; at Bonita, favour the morning; at Cosón, our spring and summer experience is pleasant swimming almost every day.

Do you need a car to visit the beaches of Las Terrenas?

For Las Ballenas, Punta Popy and Bonita, no — taxis and motoconchos are enough. For Morón, El Portillo and Rincón, a car or a driver for the day changes everything. Playa Jackson can only be reached by boat, on an excursion from Las Terrenas.

Which beach with young children?

Las Ballenas on a weekday: shallow water and restaurants within reach. The centre of Playa Rincón is also known to be calm and family-friendly. At Bonita, aim for the morning; at Cosón, simply pick a calm day and spot.
